## 4. Physics Clarification: Photons and Field Excitations

To ensure academic and physical precision, colloquial descriptions such as *"energy creates photons"* are formally replaced by established relativistic quantum field theory principles:

1. **A photon is not a separate substance created by energy.** A photon ($\gamma$) is a quantum excitation of the fundamental electromagnetic field $A_\mu$.
2. Electromagnetic radiation transports energy ($E_\gamma$) and momentum ($p_\gamma$):
   $$E_\gamma = h\nu, \qquad p_\gamma = \frac{h}{\lambda} = \frac{E_\gamma}{c}$$
3. The scientifically rigorous causal chain is formalized as:

$$\boxed{ \text{physical processes} \longrightarrow \text{electromagnetic field excitation} \longrightarrow \text{photon} \longrightarrow \text{energy/momentum transport} }$$

### Energy-to-Matter Transition: Pair Production
The transition from field radiation to massive matter is not speculative; it is a well-established phenomenon in quantum electrodynamics. Under high-energy conditions, photons interact to produce massive fermion-antifermion pairs (e.g. electron-positron pair production):

$$\gamma + \gamma \longrightarrow e^- + e^+$$

or photon-nucleus interactions:
$$\gamma + Z \longrightarrow e^- + e^+ + Z$$

Thus, the conceptual progression:
$$\text{radiation / field energy} \longrightarrow \text{massive particles}$$
is physically validated in laboratory quantum physics. EMRF investigates whether an analogous, macroscopic or cosmological mechanism operates at the interface of spacetime curvature, horizon entropy, and matter emergence.

This open formulation prevents confirmation bias and allows observational data (from S-stars, galactic rotation curves, and lensing surveys) to determine the nature of $U(X,t)$.
