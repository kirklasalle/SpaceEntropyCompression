## 1. The Bullet Cluster Empirical Challenge

In colliding galaxy clusters such as 1E 0657-56 (the "Bullet Cluster", Clowe et al. 2006):
1. **Collisional Plasma (X-ray):** The hot intra-cluster medium (ICM) gas is stripped and decelerated by hydrodynamic ram pressure, concentrating $85\%$ of the baryonic mass near the collision center.
2. **Collisionless Stellar Clumps (Optical):** The galaxies themselves collide collisionlessly, passing through to $x = -260\text{ kpc}$ and $x = +240\text{ kpc}$ ($15\%$ of baryonic mass).
3. **Weak Lensing Convergence $\kappa(x,y)$:** Lensing maps show that the gravitational potential peaks are centered on the collisionless galaxy clumps, displaced by $\sim 180 - 215\text{ kpc}$ from the dominant X-ray gas!

### Why Naive Modified Gravity Fails:
In naive modified gravity, effective gravitational mass traces baryonic mass directly:
$$\Sigma_{\text{eff}}(x,y) \propto \sqrt{\Sigma_{\text{bar}}(x,y)}$$
Because $85\%$ of the baryonic mass resides in the central gas, naive modified gravity forces the lensing peaks to remain trapped on the gas ($x_{\text{peak}} \approx 60\text{ kpc}$, offset $\approx 0\text{ kpc}$). This historical failure was cited as definitive proof of collisionless particle dark matter.
